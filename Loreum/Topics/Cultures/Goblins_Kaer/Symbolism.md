# 🧿 Goblin Symbolism
Goblin symbols are the real language they use to communicate and understand the world. Their
system of meanings is complicated and rich, allowing to express a lot of depth in fairly
small amount of symbolism.  
The art and symbols goblins create is fairly simple and primal, inspired by their environment,
but using rather few elements instead of being detailed. It is less about expressing how nature
looks, but more how nature feels.

## Understanding symbols
While drawing, goblins use symbolic depictions, but what really matters is their qualities
and relationships. No symbol exists in a vacuum - every decision goblin does have a meaning,
and these are understood intuitively by goblins as a race.

### Direction/rotation
There are four main directions shaping an object - up, down, sides and nothing. Diagonal ones
are rarely used.  
What is important to note is that opposite directions (particularly up and down) are not
considered as "opposite" meaning. They can often complement each other or add a nuance.

### Colours
Colour is an important part of erdic identity, being used from architecture, clothing, through
tapestries and tableware, to writing.  
Erds embrace the richness of colours, however they often pick some specific ones. 
The most commonly used are intense red, cream white/yellow, cream red, purple and 
dark/regular green, with darker orange and yellow being also seen from time to time.  
Blue is rare and is never dominant, pure white and black is absent pretty much.

### The Garden
One of the more common visual tropes is The Garden - depicted as three slightly rounded 
lines, portraying each level of it.  
In less abstract art, erds tend to use tree branches as a common motif, adding more meaning 
where each level “cares” about each other - the lowest by giving birth to the tree, the middle
tree by holding the top palace.

### Trees & Branches
Tree and branches motif is fairly common in many places - be it tapestries, murals or 
decorative wares.  
It is also a popular way for some sects to mark the membership - more theistic sects tend 
to create their own face tattoos that depict branches, as a way to publicly manifest their 
belief.  
Some sects tend to create their own dedicated motif that is shared between all members, 
however a lot of individuals or less dedicated sects do not hold to specific design - it is 
by no means coherent.

### Flowers
Another frequent motif is the use of flowers, specifically in ways mixing simple abstraction
with realism, as seen in art nouveau and japanese art. It's worth noting however that 
erds emphasize simplicity and balance, so their art should try to use "less is more" motto 
and rather than overstimulating with details, lead the viewer with few rich depictions of 
focus, leaving the rest of the image as rather minimal pure colour/texture.  
Girls sometimes paint flowers on their cheeks or around their eyes as a form of cheerful makeup.

### Circle of Life
Another symbol used by erdic philosophy and religion is circle of life - which is pretty 
much telling the truth of descension and ascension - the water of life descends from the 
future to the universe, and every being is slowly ascending towards death, giving back the 
life it was given.  
The symbol also includes a smaller circle that showcases the principle of mimicry - what is
the law of the universe outside, is the law of the universe inside.

### Eye
Eye is a symbol of time - the time understood how erds see it, as the past and the future. 
Just like life flows into us and as we are in the future until our eyes close, the opened 
eye represents the future. It is a symbol of knowledge that is and can be learned.
It also represents the all-seeing, the eternal nature of gardener that erdic culture 
considers as sacred obligation of everyone.  
Closed eye represents the past - everything that is no longer in the future and that ascended
the Garden. Just like the gardener takes care of the Garden, erds do the same to the 
dead - with Temple guards' armours containing a closed eye as an inverse symbolism of 
protecting sacred graveyards in Roda, Disane and Kaer's cities.

### Spiral
Spiral is a bit of an old symbol that southern races (ormaths, saphtris and erds) use for
depicting the spiritual world. It originates from ormaths, hence it has less visual 
logic than other erdic symbols and is also fairly rare nowadays within this culture.

### Halberd
The halberd is a less common symbol that encompasses ideas of safety, stability and erdic
integrity. It is sometimes also used to emphasize tradition and the longevity of erdic
culture and roots.
