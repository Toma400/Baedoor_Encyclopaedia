# 🏯 Latoka Architecture
Latoka architecture varies depending on realm, as latoka never were too homogenous culture in
itself. The styles however do not follow political alignments, being shaped more by material
conditions and historical borders.

### Coastal style
One of the more characteristic and widespread styles is **coastal** architecture, seen across
whole northern coast, from Tarsis to Kastis and ruins of Tehevel. It can also be seen in Karos, as
part of its huge cultural melting pot.

Coastal style characterises with more rounded shapes that form various smooth ledges and dome-like
tops. Buildings can have terraces or elongated parts that break from the simplest egg/onion shape,
but ultimately they are on smaller end of the stick.

They are built primarily from clay, deposits of which were easy to find on the coast and along
rivers, and painted in various colours resembling environmental richness and to blend the whiteish
walls more into the colourful landscapes. Mixes of blue, reds and orange stripes are not uncommon,
following the shape vertically.

Glass is another second resource used by coastal latoka: it is frequently used for various items, but
in terms of architecture, its common usage is for windows, doors and also unique latoka curtains
that darken their balconies from overdose sunlight. Poorer houses usually limit usage of glass
to just windows or even purely interior items.

### Monuments
While latoka architecture differ depending on location, their monuments tend to be a bit more
coherent across all areas, sharing some visual languages. The differences can be seen, but are
more subtle and revolve also heavily on focus particular realms put to spiritual paths.

Monuments have wide range of uses: from being areas of spiritual connection, to perform rites
and ceremonies, through burials, to finally scientific research or pure utility like storages.

### Tree and tunnel structures
Probably inspired by pahtri neighbours, there are few latoka structures built on trees.
In addition, latoka seem to frequently (considering how rarely this would be expected to happen)
build structures using tunnels through rocky/mountainous parts of environment.
