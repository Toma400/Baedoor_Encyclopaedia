# 🏺 Four Kings

---
| ![](/Assets/four_kings.png) |
|:---------------------------:|

**Four Kings** (or *Four Winds* for saphtri) is traditional boardgame played in Kaer and Arennan. 
It's game for four people and it uses special board, dice and four player pieces. 
It's competitive "wargame" that balances out strategy and luck with its simple rules, being played 
by people of all classes primarily to relax.

### Pieces
Four Kings is played on board containing of 17 dots, distributed geometrically on the octagonal 
shape - with each edge and vertex having one, and one located in the very middle of the board.
The dots from vertices are connected by line to the centre dot. The vertice dots are 
called "crossroads" and edge dots are simply "corners".  
The dice used for the Four Kings has six faces, with three of them containing single line going 
through, one face being blank, and two faces having dot in them.
They are called respectively "line", "blank" and "dot".

There are also four figures that are used for players. Depending on culture, they can take simpler 
or more decorative form. During the game, additional coin is frequently used to 
"jumps", however is not considered a part of game's accessory.

### Rules
At the start of the game, players put their figures on opposite vertice dots and decide the 
direction of the game - by default it's clockwise.

Players roll the dice. If their figure is on the circle (meaning every dot that is not central 
dot), the options with each dice result is following:

- **line** - you are forced to jump into next dot (based on direction chosen)
  
- **blank** - you can jump into dot behind you, or stay still
  
- **dot** - if you are on crossroad dot, you can jump into center; you can also jump into next dot, 
            or stay still

If player jumps into central dot, they must place a coin from where they jumped previously. 
They can also roll the dice for second time, which is usually the biggest advantage of such jump. 
Here however, the roll results work differently:

- **line** - you are forced to jump into next crossroad (use coin to determine)
  
- **blank** - you are forced to jump into previous crossroad
  
- **dot** - you can jump into any crossroad, or stay still
  

Once you jumped out of central dot, you can remove the coin and free it for next person getting there.

The game itself has a goal of eliminating all opponents - the last figure standing is the winner. 
Players can eliminate others by simply jumping into their position (as long as they rolled the 
dice at least once), hence why strategical usage of position and calculating possible risks is important.

### Cultural Differences
Between kehts, erds, goblins and saphtri the main difference in Four Kings is design of the 
pieces - the rules are fairly similar and so players can immediately play without need to relearn 
any new nuances.

Erdic and kehtic Four Kings are made out of wood, with octagonal board and player pieces reminding
warriors. Due to originating from this place, they are considered a classic form of this game.

Goblin variant uses circular board and can get quite decorated - and the player pieces are very 
often carved by each goblin themselves, for their own use or for someone else as a gift.  
They hold much more spiritual meaning, as they can be seen as representation of goblin, their 
family or tribe. The game itself is also seen more magically, however still holds the same main 
purpose of being a way to relax.  
The board and pieces are more often made with bone, however wood is also a possible choice.

Saphtric game is made on square board, with mechanism that lets players fold it into smaller 
square - making it much more portable companion of travels. Player pieces are also made in 
minimalistic way, as a simple cone rounded on edges, following abstract aesthetics of saphtri.
