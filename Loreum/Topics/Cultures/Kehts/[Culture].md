# 🏺 Kehtic Culture
Kehtic culture revolves around the idea of controlled dependency - it does take very different approach
to erdic and saphtric values, while keeping the general spirit of unity. It does however portray it as
more of submissive relation, thus legitimising more autocratic rule and social relations that can
be seen as more oppressive (however primarily from outside perspective).  
While kehts do focus heavily on religion, they take much more occult form, keeping spirituality and
magic as primary forms and also using that submissive character unlike **Temple of the Wheel**, for an
instance.

Despite kehts having bad press of being a heartless tyrants, their culture is much more relaxed than
one could expect from the outside. Kehtic culture suffers from being fairly strange and complex to
the ones who study it, also including catchy stereotypes, which means it's primarily erds who understand
the nuances of this culture as forever neighbours.

### Origins Influences
[Goblin origins](/Loreum/Topics/Races/Regular/Kehts.md#origins) still affect the kehts from within. 
Kehts are proud of their ascension (without arrogance towards goblins, however) in the same way 
as they were strong as Blood Tribes.
And they are subconsciously fearful of losing favour of spirits that ascended them.  
This fear shaped their culture heavily, letting submissiveness be as much a relationship with their
faith, as it is of their society. There is a nuance however that only erds and goblins understand in
this attitude, as their submissiveness is as much instinctual as it is a thrill, and a game. It is
equally a playful, performative dance that forms their spiritual identity and plays a role in even
understanding a strong bond kehts have within their societal structures.

The multitude of spirits and their use of blood rituals plays a big role in how kehtic spirituality
came into life - the vast amount of local sects is directly derived from exact spirit beings that
ascended certain tribes, and how spirits disappear and reappear, oftentimes gathering new believers
and creating schisms, raising new leaders.  
The symbolism and practicality of blood became in the same manner, equally a more abstract and more
serious part of their identity. Kehts subscribe to the blood as their way of life by the mere fact of
existence, and so they must obey this forever debt their ancestors imposed on them. The rites of
humiliation, self-harm and sacrifices follow on, however in a ways more rational and sensible than
their goblin kind did, as a heritage of their new, ascended culture.
