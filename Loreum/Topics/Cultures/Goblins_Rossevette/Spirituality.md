## Role of gods and spirits
Goblins do not believe in gods. For them, everything alive is a spirit. While they do not claim 
spirits to be inherently "evil", they are rather sceptical about trusting them.
This is why the preferred way of dealing with spirits is just existing in a way that does not 
bother them. If spirits are disinterested in a goblin tribe, this is the best outcome for everyone. 
That said, sometimes there is no way for this to happen, and so in this case, goblins try to put 
effort in either to make spirits friendly or make them go away - be it by providing them something
they want, or forcing them out, depending on the extent of demands.

Goblins usually do not perform sacrifices of tribes' members, as while this could be seen as a 
legit form of contributing to the tribe, this is considered as something not justified in most 
cases and rather wasteful.

### Protection from spirits
Goblins use various protections from spirits, trying to keep them away.  
One of more popular ones are death fences, being a group of wooden pales with heads
mounted on them, located primarily around burial sites. They do not work as a threat that anyone 
coming into them ends up being dead like the heads impaled, although it's a common misconception.
The heads impaled must be of goblin warriors of the tribe, as goblins believe spirit resides in 
the skull, and so this way warriors protect the site after death.

## Death
### Burial pitches
Burials are entirely communal - they exist within the frame of the tribe, and unless tribe moves,
it is usually built within the same area. With time, the burial area expands and can grow to 
be fairly big. Communal character provides safety for all tribe members.  
This adds one layer of context to goblins that are exiled - it puts their existence both in 
danger during their life, but also after their death.  
Burial areas are sacred for goblins, so even if tribes fight between each other, they do not 
destroy or trespass each other's burial grounds. If the tribe is annihilated due to the war, 
goblins winning should bury all their dead in their burial area, providing respective death 
fence equivalents to protect the-now-extinct tribe.

Burial areas are primarily built in two ways - open **death circles** (which don't need to be 
necessarily circles, but usually take this kind of shape) and closed **burial caves**.

![](/Assets/goblin_burial_sites.png)

They both serve the same purposes, but whether one or the other is built depends heavily on 
the environment the tribe resides in. The major feature of both objects is to have the sacred 
area (cemetary) and the death fences that protects it. If the burial area is in the open, a death
fence should be made around the entire area - in the case of a burial cave, it only needs to 
be protected on the entrance(s). Due to that lesser cost in fences, burial caves are often 
used by smaller tribes.  
Burial sites are often additionally protected by some magical patterns of stones or other equipment
with magical properties.
