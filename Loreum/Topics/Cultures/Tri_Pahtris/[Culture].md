# 🏺 Pahtric Culture
Pahtric culture is the oldest non-creature one found natively on [Baedoor Continent](/Loreum/Topics/Geography/Baedoor_Continent.md).
It got established according to pahtric creation myth at the earliest times of the world, being
a result of **Laitalu god** creating what is called **ensitri**, a tri of stars. The culture itself
has been evolving and changing for millennia, but still pretends to be faithful to its roots,
attributing it being "written in the stars" and so having ever-present, stable reference, a guide
among the tides of time.

Whatever would be the truth*, pahtric culture inherits its major part of philosophy from **Lai-Kine**,
and more precisely **The Joyous**, a particular reading of Lai-Kine teachings. According to
The Joyous, their existence is a blissful gift, and the existence - even with its hurt and pain - is
an eternal dance that is to be cherished. The Joyous is an extreme reading of it, emphasizing
role of radical involvement into the world, "digging into the soil" as it is commonly phrased, and
getting the essence out of searching that depth. This is considered a source of wisdom by pahtris
(and broader Lai-Kine for that matter), but also a form of living that is meaningful for both
individual and the community.  
Pahtris are extremely social, considering connections and shared time as part of the experience 
leading them to further understanding of Laitalu creation - and the world they were put in.
Conversation in itself is considered one of the sacred acts, and so is ascended art, being
understood as an extended form of conversation. This brings them very close to [goblins](../Goblins_Rossevette/[Culture].md)

Most of pahtric everyday life revolves around group work, meetings, discussions or parties.
Considering pahtri seamlessly merge with goblin settlements, at least on outer Rossevette, and
create a melting pot with latoka on inner, their relationships with other local civilisations
has always been very close. The openness to other races make them merge so seamlessly that
it is believed pahtric lower posture is actually a result of such liberal behaviour and interracial
breeding. It is also one of the biggest separating issues between The Joyous and Voitric teachings,
rendering pahtris as "lesser" or "tainted", "misguided" if brought friendly. For Joyous however
that is not a real problem to care of, and being named "lesser" sounds barely as an insult, rather
bringing depiction of endless tiny stars which they believe to be. The stars themselves are
after all, a symbolic sign why community serves such important part of Lai-Kine life. 

### Shadow Painters
Shadow painting is a name of a widely spread way of entertainment among pahtris and rarely
goblins, having its origins in goblins' shadow theatre.  
Pahtris have adopted shadow theatre during common encounters with goblin culture, and it became
a common practice among many, influencing pahtric culture from lowest to highest castes of society.
It also became popular way to indicate spiritual senses of The Joyous, as it helped shaping
this philosophy through simple, visual means. Skilled shadow actors were able to bring entire
scenes to the audience, giving such ephemeral stories a significant impact.  
**Shadow painting** became popular later on, as a way of magicians to collaborate with shadow 
actors. Mages let their magic colour what was previously just a shadow images, creating way more
complex and vibrant way to bring images to life. Mages who performed such illusionary colour 
follow-up to shadow actors were called shadow painters, and are popular to this day as 
either being hired by aristocrats, wandering from town to town, or as supporters of 
local religious entity. Some of them also visit goblin settlements, as goblins themselves find
this process as highly entertaining and are very welcoming to any shadow painter coming in.

Shadow painting became also an inspiration for baedoorians which gave life to first experiments
with photography in West Baedoor and attempts to create movies.

### Spiral Well
| ![](/Assets/Culture/Architecture/tri_spiral_well.png) |
|-------------------------------------------------------|

Spiral well is a structure primarily seen in centers of pahtri settlements, but also sometimes
in the wilderness, reminding a well, but with much wider circular footprint. It has stairway
leading from surface down, around the well cylinder, with occassional windows on upper parts
letting you look at above world and vines that oftenly intertwine with well's walls.  
The bottom usually stores water, or - in case of lack of it - is made into glass-like, thick 
platform that is meant to reflect the sky in absence of water. Pahtri see this part of spiral 
well as necessary to ensure Laitalu protection of buried pahtri, a blessing of above world.  
Spiral wells split every now and then into "branches" reaching outwards, creating a structure
similar to roots, in which pahtris are buried. These branches aren't usually too long or complex,
as in case of lacking space spiral wells are dug deeper. Their depth is basically a proof of how
long they were used by community.

Spiral wells are primarily dug by settlement communities, and meant as communal burial places.
In some rare cases there can be ones made by individuals, to bury their family or friends, but
these are rare and reserved to either nobility or outcasts, or any place that would have unique
reasons to do so (monasteries, fortresses).  
The structure follows the idea of "descending". From Laitalu's sky (pre-birth), through surface
(life), to the underworld (death). The connection between sky (by bottom's part) ensures Laitalu
can call the dead if they wish.
