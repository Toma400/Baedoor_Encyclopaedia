# 🎆 Necromancy

**Necromancy** is an art of turning the dead beings back to life. It is one of the more common
practices in universe, although seen by majority of common population as dangerous and 
desecratory.  
The sentiment prevails enough that there are not many books staying in the defense of necromancy
from ethical or practical point, however the diverse perspective on the topic is more common
among magic organisations and especially among necromancers themselves. A lot of them perceive
necromancy as a needed way to research aspects related to life, and some argue the process
itself can be seen in positive light.

Most of necromantic practices are kept secret, and necromant mages either do disguise as regular
mages or citizens, or leave the settled places to practice their craft in wilderness.

### Historical overview
The process of bringing dead back cannot be traced back in time, as it remains one of the oldest
categories of magic to appear.

Over time when societies started to work in more organised forms and first organisations started
to appear, slowly necromants started also collaborating with each other, creating first secret
societies. Many of first such initiatives are lost in time, not being noted by historians by their
gated character.

With expansion of scientific methods and globalisation, necromancy became a repressed interest
fascinating minds of magic adepts. Some of that could be owed to popularisation of that topic
by mages such as [Vancuerto](../../Characters/List/Vancuerto.md). Given the objections towards
necromancy are not unified across cultures, the diverse thought behind it also made it easier
for until-then taboo topic to reappear and have its status be contested.

### Ways to cover
Necromants have been hiding in most societies, usually practicing their craft alongside other
magical skills. Most necromants are found among mages, some among scholars, who use abandoned
or hidden places to work upon dead bodies. Majority of them buy the dead bodies from black
market traders or dig the graveyards. Some more research-driven ones who prefer to be ethical
try to work upon less illegal means, for example attempting to raise to life items or
creatures.

One of the ways to disguise necromants in Old Era, particularly among baedoorians and later
more broadly, was [Cornelius code](../../Characters/List/Hebarto_Cornelius.md#cornelius-code)
that used mostly normal letters and upon brief read doesn't feel different from normal writing.
Necromants have used it to pass disguised information among each other, as well as write their
research notes to not be deciphered by anyone from outside sphere.

### Negative perception
The negative sentiment against necromancy comes from two perspectives - one related more to
ethics and religion, and another from the more technical dangers coming from manipulation of
void force.

The ethical side raises the questions related to religious holiness of body ownership, as well
as the will itself.  
Several spiritual religions claim that death is caused by spirit willing to leave the body, 
being manifest of its independency, or at least that the process itself has its acceptance. 
Therefore, intruding in that process is either going against spirit's will, or even more 
strongly, enslaving it to the will of the mage. How much the intrusion happens depends on
technique, meaning that in some cases necromancy is seen as less sacrilegious if it happens
after complete death.  
If the religion assumes sacred status of the body, necromancy still remains as heretical act, 
reviving what was destined to die and be preserved. Oftentimes the point of mimicry is brought:
the revived body consists of the illusory similarity, but the life force embed in it is not
of natural spirit that lived there (or chose to, like it happens with tertens), but it appears
through void malformation of the body. The body is therefore a tool, and the spirit is either
brought to short-living existence, or entirely absent (depending again on technique, whether
it's done by evoking a spirit or through carving and binding).  
The way the dead is revived (if one can speak of the same dead at all) has big influence on
what critical points can be applied to the process, however each of these techniques have
its issues from ethical or religious point of view and cannot be done to avoid all of them.
Besides, broader public does not differentiate on such nuances and consider necromancy as
immoral regardless of details.

Technical criticism on necromancy is mainly focused on the point that trying to revive the
dead body, each step operates on pure void to some extent, rendering the action as heavily
uncontrollable and dangerous. The process itself requires a lot of magical experience, preparation
and further precision to be successful and not backfire.  
The particularly criticised part of necromancy is the idea of carving vessel, as it's a practice
that manipulates natural balance and flow of void force. It can easily increase its presence
and flow-in to the world, making it consume more of reality if incorrectly handled.

### Positive perception
The positive approach to necromancy, albeit rare, is shared by some more open mages and scholars,
as well as obviously necromants themselves.

One of the most obvious points necromancy defenders bring up is the ability to revive the beings
destined to death, thus making their life longer. This is even more valid in situation where
the spirit is preserved. A philosophical standpoint here is that the spirit leaving is not
inherently willing to, but sees no choice and accepts the fate by necessity.  
Therefore, the necromancy is virtue of keeping life, breaking the chain of nature and death.

### Techniques
When trying to revive the dead, there are certain aspects to consider performing the ritual.
The way it is executed will have different effects on the undead raised to life.

First consideration that one need to make is whether the body is meant to rise from the same
spirit that lived in it. There are certain uses from gathering particular soul, and also this
renders the necromantic effort as the least dangerous. It basically uses the powers of the
existing inhabitant of the body to raise it back to life, so the mage is only responsible for
supporting its life operating as normal.  
Using existing spirit is particularly useful for long-term undead, who would need to exist
for monthly or even yearly periods. Offloading vessel control on it is therefore very economic
on energy spent by mage, especially as it avoids artificial vessel carving that would come
if done without inhabitant.  
That said, using existing spirit needs particular steps that would enslave it within the body,
and what's even more important, it needs to happen during the death, being nearly instant
action alongside. Any negligence with fast reaction will end in spirit leaving the body and
the physical vessel to start closing - meaning that catching the spirit afterwards will
require reopening the vessel, usually involving some level of carving.

If the spirit is enslaved inside a body, nothing further is needed - the mage needs to keep it
obeying orders and maintain its living functions, but part of these aspects are handled by the
spirit trying to survive within the body.  
If however, the necromancy is performed on already dead body, the situation is somewhat
different - the vessel is then narrowed, meaning that the body is unable to be risen to life
and functions much more like an object. Therefore the required action to be taken is to carve
the vessel by yourself.

A somewhat more safe way to do it is by evoking spirit and forcing it to go into the body,
creating pseudo-terten. The spirit however may be unable to carve space for itself, or reject
it, requiring the mage to perform carving directly.  
In this case, the desired size of vessel should be carved so that the spirit can fit in, and
then the body can also rise to life, fueled this way. This renders the process much more costly
however, both in terms of single action (carving) and maintenance (keeping the spirit obedient
with body it doesn't recognise as their own).

A way to decrease these costs can be twofold - a more dangerous creation of the spirit (making
it more easily obedient, as a creation originating from mage, but then also being directly
tied, creating mystical relationship), or simple binding.  
While the first renders stronger effects and can even result in no carving performed by mage, 
the latter is a great way for creating short-term undead, as it treats the body entirely as an
object - it doesn't even have any living energy remaining, but is directly controlled by mage.  
The whole process is identical to binding any object, except for carving process that usually 
need to happen to make body have enough absorption to be animated.
