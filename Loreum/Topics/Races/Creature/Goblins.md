Goblins are creature race inhabiting [Baedoor continent](/Loreum/Topics/Geography/Baedoor_Continent.md),
specifically islands near Rossevette and Kaer. Their population spanned across Baedoor island and
Rossevette too, but it decimated heavily due to baedoorians' conquests.  
They tend to display distrust to humans and can react aggressively by default, but are not inherently
hostile if proven trustworthy.

Goblins are primarily split into five groups:
- mountainers - tribes living in northern mountain range on Baedoor Island
- wastelanders - tribes from Vershaton
- lowlanders - extinct tribes that lived on lowlands of Baedoor Island
- islanders - inhabitants of Rossevette islands
- kaerian - goblins living in Kaer woods

### Culture

While seen by many human cultures as creatures or even pests, with primitive culture not worth exploring,
goblins established quite complex society, art and beliefs.  
Their culture is isolationist from human, so the idea of "civilising" them often fails. 
That doesn't mean goblins can't be open-minded to influences or teaching, but the character of it 
can't be forced and must be trusted.

Goblins live primarily in caves and woods, constructing from small camps, through little villages to 
even fairly sized towns. The size of their settlements never reach the scope of human ones, but still
can be surprising to people understanding their culture only on its surface.

Goblins food counts primarily meat. Hunting is big part of goblins' culture and has sort of spiritual
meaning, with hunters being highly appreciated as providers of life.  
That said, there are tribes herding animals, and it is not that rare sight. Especially for goblins
living in Baedoor Island, where risk of being killed is higher.

Goblins do not write or read, their communication is built upon speech and art which is a main way
to convey ideas throughout time.  
Art serves goblins broadly, meaning even items of everyday use can have some shapes carved into
them.

Goblins do not understand human speech, but have role of translator, who is usually skilled with
languages enough and communicates between humans and goblins in those rare situations that happens.  
The only other role that understands human culture can be technicians, since they tend to learn
human writing to use that as helpful tool to improve their inventions.

### War

In war and rituals, goblins do not avoid brutality. There are certain rituals requiring death
and while in war, goblins can tear enemies apart or use any measures to annihilate them. This kind
of brutality is what feeds on stereotype of creature pest or even cannibals that should be exterminated
on sight.  
Mountainer goblins have additional military role of **paler**, who is goblin trained for stealth
and assassination, but serves also as a figure to intimidate.  
It originates from solution to goblin traitors who reported goblin tribes to baedoorian states and
bounty hunters, leading to few tribes being exterminated. Palers were meant to not only serve as
a police to such efforts, but also to brutally assassinate anyone who would try to deal with humans
against goblinfolk.  
The name of that role comes from stakes that each paler has in their inventory and uses them to mark
their target.

### History

Back in Pre Era and early Old Era, goblins from Baedoor Island were living in sort of alliance with
nomathians. This is main reason of this race having fairly good understanding of human culture
earned through lots of trades and diplomatic communication.  
Back then, goblins also were hired to nomathian army, however served there within separate units
due to their isolationist nature, being led by goblin who proved trustworthy to both sides.  
With baedoorians conquering the land, goblins were seen as subhuman species that were hired for
same reason brutal dogs could. With that forging their image, baedoorians continued with goblin
extermination and pushing them to desolate regions, feeding their hatred to humanfolk.
In the same way, after nomathians were defeated and some of their old masters assimilating in new 
reign, goblins felt betrayed as no human ally remained on their side.

### Groups
#### Mountainers
Goblins most skilled in metallurgy and technology, with also biggest cities built within caves.
They built most complex society structure and cross-race strong military alliance to defend 
themselves from baedoorians raids. This earned them ironic name of "The Goblin Society" which
appeared in [Lovrem Vantasi](/Loreum/Topics/Characters/List/Vantasi_Lovrem.md)'s book of the same name,
but which also plays to their culture seen as primitive effort to emulate civilisation.

#### Wastelanders
Inhabitants of Vershaton wastelands, they are known for being most brutal and feared. Wastelanders
live primarily in caves, but can also sometimes build camps across whole wasteland. They are way
less likely to not be hostile, perform regular raids on few settlements out there and almost
ritually massacre their victims.

#### Lowlanders
Extinct tribes that lived on Baedoor Island lowlands. They were primarily hunters and had their
small villages spread across whole area. After baedoorians' conquest, all tribes were either
exterminated or pushed to mountains, with few coastal tribes also migrating south to Rossevette
islands.

#### Islanders
Tribes living in dense woods of Karos, Veimos, Torvos and Fariva. Their culture tends to be more
settled, which results in islanders' villages and towns being biggest ones built on surface.

#### Kaerian
Kaerian goblins live in deep woods of northern and western Kaer. Both the distance from other tribes
and contacts with cultures living on the island shaped kaerian goblins to be much more involved
in rituals and contacts with spirits, which also built quite different societal structure compared
to tribes living elsewhere.
