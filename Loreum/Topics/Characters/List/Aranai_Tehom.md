**Tehom Aranai** is saphtric mage and oneironaut, and an important [oververse](/Loreum/Topics/World_Mechanics/Oververse.md)
researcher and theorist. Lived somewhen in early second century of New Era, most probably in
**Tharie**.   
Known for his discovery of Ins'in universe and concept of "rings of reality". In his works, 
he often calls for humility and curiosity as two forces that should push people towards greater
goals.  
Tehom wrote several books, most of which discuss theories related to oververse and some new ideas
of his.

### Rings of reality
While the concept of rings of reality has been circulating earlier, Aranai is known to summarise
the concept most thoroughly and popularise through few books analysing the topic.  
Tehom was also claiming that Baedoor universe is on outer ring, which built his view on many
further theories analysing oververse and its relationship to Baedoor. Eventually this concept
became fairly popular.

From these concepts comes also the idea of oververse topography, a symbolic representation of
universes' relationship to the Void showcased as a constellation. The Void, as an energy seeping
from outside black to the white absence (which is world) creates the metaphysical idea of
"outer" spaces (rings) where the absorption of void is higher and more supernatural phenomena
happens, abrupting rules of the world more commonly. That space however is gradiental and the
closer to the interior of that territory, the more resistant the universes become, making their
rules more stable and magic taking more invisible, indirect form.

### "Oververse is unknowness" and Aranai's principles
Across his oneironautic career, Tehom was said to be enthusiastic and supportive, often
emphasising role of both the passion, curiosity, but also a balancing humility that helps
to consider the research through more rational lens, knowing limitation of researcher.

A good depiction of his values is quote written in [Starshifters'](/Loreum/Topics/Guilds/List/Starshifters.md)
notes from meeting held in 136 NE:
> We know fraction of what this concept really is. We don't know gods, ourselves, we barely 
> touch knowledge of worlds. And who knows if even gods understand oververse, if they created it, 
> do creator know the child? Do they know themselves, or is it barely part of what they think 
> of themselves?  
And what is this outside of them, if there wasn't before? This is why I'm leaning towards 
> thought that gods were in oververse, but didn't create it. If it ever was created, or 
> started existing, as it transcends every concept we had, so it could as well exist in a ways 
> we couldn't describe with our language, even imagine in our minds.  
Oververse is unknowness, and this is the most we learn from our everyday journeys: the more 
> you wander, the more there's to discover.

The quote points out rightfully that the knowledge known to baedoorians is extremely limited - not
because of them being ignorant, but rather as awareness of it being infinite and hard to penetrate.
Oneironauts in particular understood this problem, as the concept of oververse in itself was
and still is one of the most complex topics known to baedoorian cosmology.

Tehom scepticism towards knowledge rendered him as softly dismissing some religious' beliefs
and their certainty, however being raised in saphtric faith, he understood the meaning of
symbolical reality of myths and thin thread connecting them to physical world.  
That said, his quote has struck the chord due to some of its passages seen as heretical, in
particular idea of gods being more humane, and more dependent on outside forces than creators
of them.  
The passage "as it transcends every concept we had" also points out Tehom was most likely
a believer of Higher Oververse.
