# 🈳 Ghloddish Alphabet
Ghloddish alphabet is pretty complicated to learn, as it can be portrayed in semi-latin way, yet it
has a lot of aspects which make it not intuitive. This is why it is rather recommended to write it
using ghloddish glyphs.  
For glyph writing, each ghloddish vocabulary has separate section, **Letters**, explaining glyphs
creating the word.

Second era added `y` letter, which was crucial for further evolution of the language. Additionally, 
"ending" letters became simply consonants and were treated more like so.

### III Era
Third era is known for addition of a lot of needless consonants which were never really used aside
from handling "imported" names. As much as those does irritate everyone, purists portray them as
a proof of ghloddish language being modern.

|            |      Doubled       |         Single         |
|------------|:------------------:|:----------------------:|
| Hissing    |     th, gh, sh     |                        |

| Vowels     |                    |       e, o, a, s       |


### IV Era
Fourth era is seamless update for third one, focused mostly on comeback to double-letters `t*`, as
well as deprecating consonants being out of use. A lot of fourth era ghloddish users try to
translate foreign names using old alphabet, saying this is truer way of being a ghlodd - not being
dominated by outlanders, but fitting them into your scope.

## Understanding the letters

### Extending apostrophe
Extending apostrophe is core aspect of ghloddish alphabet. In latin transliteration it is portrayed
as `'`, and is added after specific letter - `a'` - or doubled letter - `th'`. In glyph writing, it is
simply `|` put after the letter.

In speaking, apostrophe extends the last sound of the letter before it - and as much as [double letters are separate](Alphabet.md#doubled-letters), 
this refer also to second part of the doubled letter (in glyph writing it is rather obvious).

To show why is this crucial aspect of ghloddish language, let me show you its uses:
- shorten repetitive same-letters (``Baedoor → Paedo'r``)
- after ending the syllable with vowel (``Uershaton -> Uersha'ton``)
- as epithet, appending attribute to something (``Y'she'o'd = God of Fire``)

Apostrophes generally are deprecated during words evolution, so in many cases their amount is lowering
with each era, fourth being peak of it. Aside from general trend, they have several rules which are
deprecating them even during word creation, such as:
- When new word originates from epithet, apostrophes generally tend to lose themselves, the ones being
  in place of double same-letters mostly surviving
- If new word would have double apostrophe, it simply gets apostrophe at the end of whole word, and
  all such cases in the word are usually ommited from this point
- Still, if apostrophes are overflowing the word, the same system is used, usually resulting on words
  getting shortest variant possible - this is also the rule which is the most evident during language
  evolution.

Additionally, ghloddish usage of extending apostrophes have also two in-depth aspects:
- Doubled letters **use** apostrophe within itself as mirror, therefore `a` and `oa` will look 
  different - first of those is single letter, second is 'mirrored' letter (mirrored from the apostrophe)
- Extending apostrophe is treated as half-letter, therefore there can be word differing from another
  just by use of this apostrophe - the difference in sound is barely hearable for foreigners

### Doubled letters
Doubled letters are letters using two glyphs put around apostrophe, in mirrored way. In short, those
letters are dominant ones in words, as if there's any confusion on how to write the word, we should
choose option using doubled letters. This can be sometimes tricky (especially with unfamous `ta'hr`
containing `ta-ah-r`, with only third era allowing `t-a-ah-r` writing) but relies mostly on getting
used to writing in this particular language.
