        someone local in such weird place. Secondly, because it fits through all aspects except
        for somewhat uncharacteristic legs. The weird thing about Nasvioh is also that he's the
        one to sell passing permit, but this is not unusual for BaeCL and this notebook in
        particular.<br>
        The second person, a guest of Nasvioh, is called Doctor Anterkiritt. The name is somewhat
        unusual, and while it doesn't strike heavy baedoorian origins, it feels somewhat close.
        The race of the doctor would also hit home - that said however, I think it would be
        more interesting to consider this as latoka name, given to early baedoorian settlers
        who married latoka. This would explain physiognomy and higher education, yet a name
        that doesn't strike baedoorian at first - my thinking is that Anterkiritt is a mixture
        of two other names, Anter and Kiritt. Both sounds fairly latokaish to me.<br>
        Last but not least, I think the statue is somewhat of an important part of latoka culture
        peek. It has writings, and also - aside of love for statues - it tells us that such

### Lore accuracy
Very high lore accuracy, with only minimal nitpicks that do not follow established canon or broader
economic sense. So most notably the weird 1096 (A.D.?) date, economy built on diamonds and
transformations of coal to these.
Few renames were done to ensure more creative feel to what was mostly generic region naming.
